Abstract

Recent advances in video generative models have enabled high-fidelity, temporally coherent video generation. However, these models often struggle to satisfy prompts requiring specialized knowledge, specific identities, physical consistency, or ordered events. In this paper, we present VideoGen-Agent, a multimodal agent trained through multitask agentic reinforcement learning to use external tools for video generation. The agent coordinates augmentation, generation, and verification tools through multi-turn interactions, using the prompt and intermediate observations to guide its decisions. We train a shared policy on a category-balanced dataset spanning six tasks. Supervised fine-tuning on teacher-generated trajectories establishes tool-use behavior, which is then refined through reinforcement learning. A category-aware hybrid reward evaluates tool-call validity, task-appropriate tool use, and generated video quality. We further introduce VABench, a held-out benchmark of 600 prompts designed to evaluate video generation agents across six capability categories: procedural knowledge, single- and multi-entity identity preservation, physical consistency, scene composition, and multi-shot temporal structure. On VABench, VideoGen-Agent improves over its base video generator by 19.1 points, from 56.5 to 75.6. Upgrading the generation tools further raises the score to 86.1 without additional agent training. In a paired human evaluation, raters prefer VideoGen-Agent with Toolset 1 over its underlying standalone text-to-video generator in 84.3% of comparisons. These results support learning tool use across video-generation tasks and show that the trained agent can benefit from subsequent advances in generation tools.

Method

VideoGen-Agent formulates video generation as a multi-turn tool-use problem. Given a prompt, the agent sequentially decides whether to seek additional context, generate a video candidate, or verify and refine an intermediate result. Tools are organised into three functional groups:

1 · Augmentation

Text web search, image search, and code-based physical simulation ground generation in external knowledge and controllable priors.

2 · Generation

A family of video generators with different conditionings: T2V, I2V, motion-conditioned, and (multi-) reference-image-to-video.

3 · Verification

Open-vocabulary detection and depth estimation inspect generated videos for subject localisation, spatial relations, and geometric consistency.

Training

Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, we train on 16K teacher trajectories from Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 4.7, then apply multitask GRPO on a separate set of 8K prompts. The 24K prompts span six task categories. The hybrid reward combines tool-call validity, category-specific video quality, and task-appropriate tool use, with weights 0.1, 0.5, and 0.4. Group-relative advantage normalization is followed by task-level normalization.

SFT uses two epochs with a learning rate of 5 × 10−5; RL uses one epoch at 10−6 and six usable rollouts per prompt. Failed action spans are masked during SFT. During RL, trajectories that fail because of external tool errors are resampled before group statistics are computed.

VABench

VABench is a held-out benchmark designed to evaluate video generation agents on complex tasks across six capabilities, with 600 prompts and 100 per category. Prompts undergo repetition checks, VLM filtering, and manual selection. Evaluation uses Gemini 3.1 Pro with the same category-specific rubrics and weights as the RL video reward; all 600 prompts contribute to the reported means.

Procedural Knowledge (PK)

Named techniques / processes whose execution depends on specific motion details.

Single-Entity Identity (SI)

One named subject, such as a game character, celebrity, landmark, or branded object; visual identity must be preserved across the clip.

Multi-Entity Identity (MI)

Multiple named subjects; identity preservation under interaction.

Physics Simulation (PS)

Quantitative physical motion under specified initial conditions, restricted to collisions and falling/dropping in training and VABench.

Compositional Scene (CS)

Multi-subject composition, interactions, and spatial relations using visually canonical subjects to focus on scene structure.

Multi-Shot (MS)

Two or three equal-duration shots, each at most five seconds, with all requested phases depicted in the correct temporal order.

The fixed-workflow baseline uses the same Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone and Toolset 1 without task-specific training. For MI, the backbone receives the required entity count N, lists N names, retrieves one reference image per entity, and calls video_gen_multiple_reference for reference-to-video generation. For CS, ReAct is capped at three iterations; other tasks execute their prescribed workflow once. The paper appendix details these task-specific budgets and the single-task training setup.

Prompt rewriting improves the overall score from 56.5 to 57.3, while the fixed workflow reaches 64.1. SFT further improves performance to 69.2, and RL adds 6.4 points to reach 75.6, with gains across all six categories. The advantage over the fixed workflow supports the usefulness of training under these task-specific baseline budgets.

Removing the VLM reward reduces the overall score to 73.3, while removing the tool reward lowers it to 71.2; both ablations degrade all six categories. Single-task agents undergo SFT and then RL using only their category-specific subsets, with other settings unchanged. They achieve 76.3 overall, 0.7 points above the shared multitask agent. Single-task training performs better on PK, MI, and CS, while multitask training leads on SI, PS, and MS and supports all six tasks with a single policy at comparable overall performance.

Generalization to an unseen task combination

Procedural Identity (PI) combines procedural knowledge with single-entity identity. We construct 100 held-out prompts from 20 specialized actions or processes and 20 named entities, disjoint from SFT, RL, and VABench prompts. No training prompt combines these requirements. The system prompt provides general composition guidance, but no explicit workflow for this combination.

Human evaluation and judge validation

Human preference: four raters compare VideoGen-Agent-Toolset 1 with Seedance 1.0 Pro Fast on 100 randomly sampled VABench video pairs, with no tie option. Across 400 judgments, the preference rate for Toolset 1 is 84.3%, rounded to one decimal place.

Judge–human agreement: a separate study uses 100 pairs per category (600 total), balanced across the strongest standalone baseline, SFT, and RL. Three blinded raters independently assess each pair, and Gemini 3.1 Pro agrees with their majority preference on 92.0% of pairs. Equal VLM scores trigger a separate forced-choice comparison under the same rubric; these choices are used for agreement, not to replace benchmark scalar scores.

Robotic manipulation with an additional tool family

Starting from the RL-trained checkpoint, we fine-tune the policy on 1,000 additional robotic-manipulation examples. Given an input image and instruction, the agent calls π0.5 to predict a joint-action trajectory and passes the actions to Ctrl-World to generate a video. These qualitative examples show that the fine-tuned policy can invoke the two tools in sequence. This robotics checkpoint is separate from the checkpoint evaluated on the six video-generation tasks.

The four examples cover placing a blue or red block in a white plate, folding a towel, and moving a towel from right to left. Columns show the prompt, input image, ground-truth recording, VideoGen-Agent output, and Wan 2.5 I2V output.
