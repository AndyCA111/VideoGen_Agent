VideoGen-Agent:
Reinforcing Video Generation Agents

TL;DR. VideoGen-Agent learns to dynamically compose search, physics simulation, video generation, and visual verification tools into prompt-specific workflows. Trained through supervised tool-use learning followed by agentic reinforcement learning, it improves Seedance 1 Pro Fast from 56.5 to 75.6 on VABench. Upgrading the generation backend to Seedance 2 at inference time, without retraining the policy, further raises the score to 84.7, demonstrating transferable tool-use capabilities across video-generation backends.

Abstract

Recent advances in video generative models have enabled the generation of high-fidelity, temporally coherent videos. However, they remain fundamentally constrained by frozen internal knowledge, weak physical reasoning, and absent temporal scheduling, often failing on real-world prompts that name specific entities, require physically plausible dynamics, or prescribe ordered phases. In this paper, we present VideoGen-Agent, the first attempt to train an agentic reinforcement learning framework for video generation that performs multi-step reasoning and tool use to ground every aspect of the generation process. To achieve this, we first construct an agentic text-to-video training dataset with diverse, category-balanced prompts across six tasks, each targeting a distinct capability gap in current models. Using this dataset, we train VideoGen-Agent with supervised fine-tuning (SFT), followed by reinforcement learning (RL) via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), guided by a category-aware hybrid reward that combines tool format, tool use quality and vision language model (VLM) as video-judge signals to provide stable and informative supervision for tool-use agentic rollouts. We further introduce VABench, a held-out benchmark of 600 prompts that explicitly evaluates search-grounded knowledge, identity preservation, physical plausibility, and multi-phase temporal reasoning. Experiments show that VideoGen-Agent brings substantial gains, improving the video generation baseline by around 19 points on VABench. We hope this work can serve as an open foundation for tool use video generation agents.

Method

VideoGen-Agent formulates video generation as a multi-turn tool-use problem. Given a prompt, the agent sequentially decides whether to seek additional context, generate a video candidate, or verify and refine an intermediate result. Tools are organised into three functional groups:

1 · Augmentation

Text web search, image search, and code-based physical simulation ground generation in external knowledge and controllable priors.

2 · Generation

A family of video generators with different conditionings: T2V, I2V, motion-controlled (ControlNet), and (multi-) reference-image-to-video.

3 · Verification

Grounding DINO and Depth Anything inspect generated videos for subject localisation, spatial relations, and geometric consistency.

Training

Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, we first distil teacher trajectories via supervised fine-tuning, then optimise the agent with GRPO using a category-aware hybrid reward that combines format correctness, model-as-judge video score, and tool-quality signals. This gives stable, informative gradients for multi-turn rollouts.

VABench

Procedural Knowledge (PK)

Named techniques / processes whose execution depends on specific motion details.

Single-Entity Identity (SI)

One named real-world subject; identity must be preserved across the clip.

Multi-Entity Identity (MI)

Multiple named subjects; identity preservation under interaction.

Physics Simulation (PS)

Physically governed motion under specified initial conditions: collisions, free fall, orbits.

Compositional Scene (CS)

Multi-entity composition, inter-object interaction, spatial reasoning.

Multi-Shot (MS)

Explicit time-ordered sequences with complete and correctly ordered phases across multiple shots.

Beyond text‑to‑video — embodied manipulation

The same agentic pattern transfers to robot manipulation. Given a natural-language instruction, the agent (1) calls pi0.5 to predict a 60-step joint trajectory, then (2) hands that trajectory to ctrl-world, an action-conditioned world model that renders the resulting rollout. Unlike pure I2V baselines that see only the first frame, the agentic stack consumes the policy's planned actions — so the synthesized video is grounded in a real motor plan, not imagined.
